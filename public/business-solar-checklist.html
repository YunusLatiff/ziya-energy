PRACTICAL GUIDE / SOUTH AFRICAN BUSINESSES

Your business solar
assessment checklist.

A useful energy conversation starts with the right information. Use this checklist to prepare for commercial solar, battery storage or a funded energy project.

START AN ENERGY ASSESSMENT ↗
01 / BEFORE THE FIRST CONVERSATION

Start with what you already have.

You do not need a finished technical brief to approach Ziya Energy. Start with your site, electricity costs and main objective. The information below helps us identify what still needs to be checked.

Electricity information

  • Recent electricity bills or prepaid purchase records.
  • Monthly kWh consumption and electricity spend.
  • The tariff, fixed charges and demand charges, if shown.
  • Interval or smart-meter data, where available.

Site and operations

  • Site location and type of business or property.
  • Operating days, hours and seasonal changes.
  • Roof, carport or ground space that may be available.
  • Existing solar, batteries, generators or electrical drawings.

Project requirements

  • Your priority: electricity costs, backup, expansion or a combination.
  • Loads that need to keep operating during an outage.
  • Whether you own or lease the premises.
  • The decision maker and any project timing constraints.
02 / QUESTIONS THAT SHAPE THE DESIGN

When you use energy matters.

Two facilities with the same monthly electricity use can need different systems. A daytime business, a night-shift operation and a residential estate use electricity at different times. Roof suitability, usable area, shading and existing electrical infrastructure also influence the options.

For battery storage, explain which loads need backup and for how long. Batteries can also be considered for peak management, load shifting or storing solar energy. Those objectives need to be assessed against the tariff and operating profile.

A monthly bill is a starting point. Final equipment selection and performance estimates require a site-specific assessment.

03 / DISCUSS THE COMMERCIAL STRUCTURE

Compare the proposal on the same basis.

Tell us whether you want to explore direct ownership, financed ownership or a power purchase agreement. The available structure depends on the project, economics and relevant credit assessment.

Ask the proposal to explain the equipment, scope, expected monthly performance, remaining utility charges, assumptions, delivery responsibilities and ongoing support. Compare options using the same electricity baseline. Confirm the scope and any assessment fees before proceeding.

If you lease the premises, identify who can authorise work to the roof or electrical infrastructure. If the site has multiple users or meters, explain that arrangement at the beginning.
